This is the exact proof of the DBX concourse model we established in Round One.

England scores early, and Tuchel immediately collapses the team into a back five. What is a back five? It is the deliberate destruction of the 2D passing lattice. England creates a high-density bottleneck in the center—Henry calls it "traffic". They are trying to force Argentina into the Plane Train, a zero-variance ballistic channel where attacks go to die.

If Argentina tries to ballistic (ATL plane train) through that center, they fail, because England has removed all the neighbors from the graph. The stochastic search collapses.

So what does Scaloni do? He looks at the topology and abandons the center. He moves Messi to the right flank. He explicitly chooses the DBX concourse.

As mapped in the previous artifact: DBX concourse = 1D random walk, local reward sampling. Football analogue: Wing dribbling, individual duels.

By isolating Messi 1v1 on the right, Scaloni shifts the algorithm. Messi isn't playing tiki-taka; he is walking the 1D duty-free tunnel, stopping at the full-back's storefront, testing the barrier, and generating high-variance crosses. He bypasses the traffic entirely.

the learning rate of panic

Zlatan highlights the hyperparameter collapse perfectly: "Tuchel made some changes he went too defensive... England stopped playing when they scored the goal."

England's shift to a back five was a catastrophic error in optimization. When you score and immediately "sit in," you drop your learning rate (η) to zero. You stop exploring the loss landscape and hope the current basin is deep enough to survive the remaining time.

Argentina, conversely, maintained their stochasticity. As Zlatan notes, "they didn't panic, they kept attacking." They didn't take massive, ballistic risks. They just relocated their search from the congested center to the uncongested edge. The two goals in six and a half minutes weren't a Route One miracle; they were the inevitable result of a system that kept its variance alive against a system that intentionally froze itself.
